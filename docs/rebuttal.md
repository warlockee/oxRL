   | Qwen2.5-32B | GSM8K (%) | Δ vs Base |
   |---|---|---|
   | Base (Instruct) | 80.52 | — |
   | SP-RFT | 80.59 | +0.07 |
   | DPO | 82.26 | +1.74 |

   Three findings. (i) **The inversion persists — and completes — at 32B**: the small-scale ranking is now fully reversed end-to-end. Self-play SFT has nothing left to learn from at this scale, while contrastive objectives still extract signal. (iii) The SimPO result echoes the paper's 14B finding that SimPO surges on harder regimes at large scale (39.1% on MATH at 14B), reinforcing that small-scale rankings are anti-predictive precisely for the methods practitioners would discard early. ## Questions

**Q1 — Missing values in Table 1.** The "---" cells are IPO/KTO/SimPO at 3B. They were omitted because the 3B default-LR pathology made every method's strict-match score uninformative, and the dedicated 3B LR sweep covered SP-RFT and DPO (the axis of the paper's central claim); SimPO was explicitly listed as unswept in Limitations. We have now run the missing cells at the shared default LR (3 seeds each, GSM8K 8-shot strict-match): **IPO 24.92 ± 0.85, KTO 23.68 ± 0.87, SimPO 9.60 ± 3.84** — completing the controlled-comparison column. The pattern matches the rest of the column: every method sits far below the base model's flexible-extract ability (62.9%), confirming the pathology is checkpoint-wide rather than method-specific, with SimPO again the most fragile. We will also add a table note stating the reason for any remaining "—" (SGRPO at 14B: ~6× rollout compute, stated in §4.7).

**Q2 — Will the inversion hold for newer models (Qwen3)?** We cannot claim this without running it, and we deliberately do not. What the paper offers instead is a mechanism with a testable implication: Finding 2 shows initialization (prior alignment of the Instruct checkpoint) dominates loss choice (spread compression 15.7→1.07 pp at 1.5B, 6.00→1.82 pp at 7B), and Finding 3 shows the small-scale differences are format-compliance effects. Qwen3's instruct initialization differs substantially from Qwen2.5's (hybrid thinking mode, different alignment recipe), so our framework predicts the *initialization* effect will again dominate the *algorithm* effect — a falsifiable prediction we will state in the Discussion. The Gemma-3 replication is the evidence we can offer that the phenomenon is not tied to one family's pretraining recipe.

**Q3 — Why 5 seeds in some experiments and 3 in others?** Seed budget followed the statistical load of each claim: N=5 where the headline significance tests live (7B core methods SP-RFT/DPO/SimPO — the inversion claim, p<0.001 after Bonferroni; and the 20-variant study at 1.5B = 100 runs), N=3 for the supporting grids (1.5B core, IPO/KTO at 7B, 14B, Gemma, controls), N=1 only for clearly-marked exploratory cells (footnoted in Table 1). Per-cell N is printed in every table; we will add one sentence in §3 stating this policy. Total: ~350 runs, ~940 GPU-hours (Appendix Compute).

**Q4 — Zero variance at 0.5B; no variance for base.** Two conventions plus one genuine finding, and we can now give a complete answer:
- *Base rows:* the ± column reports variance across **training seeds**; base models undergo no training, so a single deterministic greedy evaluation is reported without ±.
- *The σ=0 cells at 0.5B:* these are real, separately-trained, separately-evaluated runs that produced bit-identical accuracies. The cause is now precisely understood: in the framework version used for the March runs, the training-data sampler received a fixed default seed, so runs differing only in `run.seed` saw identical data order; with deterministic bf16 training and greedy decoding, training is bit-reproducible — the per-run seed affected only components unused in offline training. This was fixed in oxRL v1.9.0 (the sampler now consumes `run.seed`), and the 1.5B/7B tables in the paper already use post-fix reruns with genuine variance (Appendix Multi-Seed Verification). The 0.5B column retained pre-fix runs, documented as "training determinism" in the appendix but without the mechanism — the reviewer is right to flag it.
- *New experiment:* we re-ran the **entire 0.5B grid** (5 algorithms × 3 seeds) under the fixed seed mechanism, evaluated under the same protocol as Table 1 (GSM8K 0-shot CoT, strict-match, greedy):

  | Algorithm | Original (deterministic) | Rerun (seeded, N=3) |
  |---|---|---|
  | SP-RFT | 33.97 ± 0.0 | **33.59 ± 0.26** |
  | DPO | 33.97 ± 0.0 | **33.18 ± 0.31** |
  | IPO | 34.50 ± 0.3 | **33.97 ± 0.68** |
  | KTO | 33.81 ± 0.0 | **33.38 ± 0.98** |
  | SimPO | 26.08 ± 0.0 | **23.55 ± 5.19** |

  Three take-aways: (i) with the seed correctly consumed by the data sampler, variance is genuine and nonzero (σ = 0.26–0.98 for the stable methods); (ii) the original point estimates were accurate to <1 pp, so no conclusion changes; (iii) the exact SP-RFT = DPO coincidence in the original column was an artifact of the deterministic pipeline and disappears under seeded reruns. SimPO's σ = 5.19 independently replicates the paper's SimPO-instability finding (Appendix SimPO Variance) at a third scale. We will replace the 0.5B column with these numbers and document the mechanism in the appendix.
