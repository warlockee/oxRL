# Confidential comment to the Area Chair

Dear AC — thank you for the metareview. We would like to respectfully note that **each substantive concern it raises was already addressed with new experiments during the discussion period**, posted across the three reviewer threads before the metareview deadline. Because the evidence is distributed over three responses, we summarize the mapping here, with all per-seed numbers verifiable in the anonymized artifact repository: https://anonymous.4open.science/r/nips-dnb-artifact-2026-883E (`results/RESULTS_SUMMARY.md`).

**1. "GSM8K possibly saturated and formatting-sensitive."** Not saturated under our protocol: base strict-match is 75.8 (7B), 79.5 (14B), 80.5 (32B), with post-training reaching 87.4. Formatting does not explain the deployment-scale inversion: it persists under flexible extraction (7B: DPO 84.99 vs SP-RFT 80.29, +4.7 pp with format removed from the metric — submission Appendix, Table format_gap), and replicates on MATH (14B: SP-RFT last; Gemma-12B: DPO +9 pp).

**2. "Format-compliance explanation lacks error rates / item-level breakdowns."** We ran exactly this analysis (Reviewer 2 thread): per-item logs at 1.5B (5 algorithms × 3 seeds) give format-error rates of 1.26–5.66% that track strict-rank deficits (SimPO's 5.66% accounts for ~half its deficit), and strict solve-set Jaccard overlaps of 0.65–0.89 — algorithms largely solve the same items and differ at the margin, as the format-compliance regime predicts.

**3. "Only two families; MATH suggests architecture-specific behavior."** We added a third family during the discussion (Llama-3.2-1B / Llama-3.1-8B, 3 seeds, identical protocol). Result, reported plainly: at 8B the trend *reverses* (SP-RFT 75.97±0.72 > base 74.00 > DPO 71.82±1.03). The revision embraces this: the specific inversion direction is scoped to Qwen and Gemma, and the general claim is the one all three families support — scale-trends are architecture-dependent, so neither small-scale nor cross-family results predict deployment behavior. This *strengthens* the paper's practitioner warning rather than weakening it.

**4. "Largest scale is modest."** We added Qwen2.5-32B at N=3 seeds (Reviewer 1 thread): SimPO 83.42±1.14 > DPO 82.23±0.04 > SP-RFT 80.82±0.23 ≈ base 80.52 (DPO > SP-RFT at p<0.01). SP-RFT's gain over base collapses to +0.30±0.23 — statistically zero — completing the RFT-ceiling trajectory (+23.2 pp at 1.5B → +1.6 at 7B → +0.3 at 32B), with the mechanism directly measurable in the data pipeline (pair-yield falls 85.5% → 10.9% from 0.5B to 32B).

**5. "Fixed-dataset control addresses only one direction."** We ran the reverse control (Reviewer 3 thread): 7B trained on 1.5B-generated data, N=3. SP-RFT rises to parity with DPO (82.26±0.68 vs 81.68±0.98) — the data regime does modulate the gap, and the revision states the sharpened conclusion: the inversion is jointly driven by scale and the self-play data regime. A teacher-data sweep at the 1.5B learner (data from 0.5B/own/7B/14B; SP-RFT ≥ DPO at every teacher scale) completes the factorial margin.

**6. "LoRA/full-FT split without adequate ablation."** The 7B full-FT factorial is now N=3 seeds (was single-seed): DPO 83.85±0.85, SimPO 83.22±1.41, SP-RFT 79.35±0.77 — a 4.5 pp DPO−SP-RFT separation mirroring the LoRA cells.

**7. Generative-benchmark scope (raised by R3).** We additionally evaluated IFEval (generative, judge-free): ≤1.7 pp spread among trained models, no inversion — the divergences are confined to mathematical reasoning, now shown beyond multiple-choice formats.

**8. Presentation and template.** All presentation issues are already fixed in the revised manuscript pushed with the responses (abstract/introduction threshold unified to ≤3B; missing 3B cells filled with 3-seed runs; 0.5B column replaced with corrected multi-seed values and the seed-determinism mechanism documented; naming normalized). We will ensure the camera-ready uses the official NeurIPS 2026 template verbatim.

In total the discussion period added ~220 training runs and ~300 evaluations (32B ×3 seeds, a third model family, reverse and factorial controls, item-level analyses), all released with per-seed JSONs. Reviewer 3 (borderline accept) asked precisely for the reverse control, multi-seed factorial, and a generative benchmark — all delivered.
