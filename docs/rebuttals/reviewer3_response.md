**M4 — Mechanistic evidence beyond the narrative.** Two quantitative additions ground the RFT-ceiling account. (a) *Pass-rate composition across scales* (also the reviewer's Q4): the fraction of GSM8K training prompts yielding informative preference pairs falls monotonically with scale in every family — Qwen: 85.5% (0.5B) → 88.5% (1.5B) → 24.7% (7B) → 17.8% (14B) → 10.9% (32B); Gemma: 58.0% (1B) → 33.9% (12B); Llama-3: 89.1% (1B) → 51.0% (8B). The reviewer's suspicion is correct that effective dataset composition shifts with scale — this is inherent to the practitioner self-play workflow we benchmark, and it is exactly the confound the fixed-dataset, gold-data, and (new) reverse controls isolate. (b) *Item-level analysis at 1.5B* (3 seeds, per-item logs): format-error rates (flexible-correct ∧ strict-wrong) of 1.3–5.7% track strict-rank deficits, and strict solve-sets overlap heavily across algorithms (Jaccard 0.65–0.89) — methods largely solve the same items and differ at the margin, as the format-compliance regime predicts. We agree that gradient-norm / loss-landscape probes would be stronger still and scope them as future work in the Discussion.

**Weakness 3 — Shared hyperparameters for variants.** The conclusion is deliberately scoped to the *plug-and-play* setting (the revision states this explicitly): the claim is that variants provide no reliable out-of-the-box gains, not that tuned ceilings are equal. Two checks bound the risk: β sweeps for the two strongest 1.5B variants (DPOP: 54.7/55.2/53.3; ORPO: 54.8/54.4/53.8 at β ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 0.5}) move results ≤1.9 pp, and LR sweeps at 1.5B/3B/7B preserve the core rankings at every tested LR including per-algorithm optima.

**Q1 — Prompt-format sensitivity.** All evaluations use the identical LM-Eval-Harness prompt across families, so the evaluation side is held fixed; training data, however, is formatted with each model's own chat template (the practitioner workflow). We cannot fully deconfound template-algorithm interaction, and we will note this in Limitations. The strict-vs-flexible analysis bounds the concern at deployment scale: the 7B inversion persists under flexible extraction (+4.7 pp), so it is not an artifact of answer-format parsing.

**Q2 — LoRA rank at Gemma-12B.** Honest answer: r=16/α=32 was inherited from the Qwen setup, not separately validated for Gemma. The default LR was validated per-family only insofar as it matches both families' official fine-tuning guidance; a Gemma-side rank/LR sweep is noted as a limitation in the revision.

**Q3 — The MATH architecture reversal.** Our working hypothesis, stated as testable rather than established: SimPO's length-normalized, reference-free objective interacts with family-specific output-length and formatting distributions (Qwen-14B MATH responses are longer and benefit from length normalization; Gemma's stronger prior formatting favors reference-anchored updates). The item-level tooling released with the artifacts supports testing this; we prefer not to overclaim beyond the data.

**Q4 — Pass-rate distributions.** Yes — quantified above under M4(a); per-scale parquets with per-prompt outcomes are in the artifact repository.
