# Response to Reviewer 3

We thank the reviewer for a careful and constructive review — the summary is precise, and we especially appreciate the recognition of the control design and honest scoping. Below we address each point; three of the four major comments are answered with **new experiments run during the discussion period**. All numbers (per-seed) are verifiable in the anonymized artifact repository: https://anonymous.4open.science/r/nips-dnb-artifact-2026-883E (`results/RESULTS_SUMMARY.md`; raw eval JSONs in `results/`; frozen configs in `release/configs/`) — this also addresses the Reproducibility: Partly assessment.

**M1 — Generative benchmark gap.** We agree, and we took both actions the reviewer proposed. (a) *A generative datapoint*: we evaluated the released 7B full-FT checkpoints on **IFEval** — generative instruction following with rule-based (judge-free) verification. Prompt-level strict accuracy: base 73.01, DPO 71.53, SP-RFT 70.24, SimPO 69.87 — a ≤1.7 pp spread among trained models with no inversion, plus a small uniform cost of math-specialization vs base (≈1.5–2.8 pp). This extends the task-specificity finding beyond multiple-choice log-likelihood benchmarks to a generative instruction-following benchmark: the large, scale-dependent divergences we report are confined to mathematical reasoning. (b) *Prominence*: the revision now states the generative-benchmark limitation in the abstract and introduction, not only §4.4 and the Conclusion. AlpacaEval/MT-Bench require LLM-judge infrastructure we could not stand up within the discussion window; we commit to at least one judge-based benchmark for the camera-ready.

**M2 — The reverse fixed-dataset control.** An excellent gap; we ran it: **Qwen-7B trained on 1.5B-generated (low-quality, abundant) self-play data**, SP-RFT vs DPO, 3 seeds each, full-FT protocol (GSM8K 8-shot strict-match):

| 7B learner trained on | SP-RFT | DPO |
|---|---|---|
| own (7B) data | 79.35 ± 0.77 | **83.85 ± 0.85** |
| 1.5B-teacher data | 82.26 ± 0.68 | 81.68 ± 0.98 |

The reviewer's hypothesis is **partially confirmed, and we report it as a refinement**: on abundant weak-teacher data (6,614 pairs vs 1,848 from the 7B's own saturated self-play), SP-RFT rises to statistical parity with DPO — data composition does modulate the 7B gap. However, the inversion is not restored (no SP-RFT advantage emerges), and the best 7B configuration remains DPO-on-own-data. The revision will state the sharpened conclusion: the 7B inversion is jointly driven by scale and the self-play data regime — SP-RFT's collapse on own-data is the RFT ceiling made visible (pass-rate table below), while DPO's lead is robust to data source but not amplified by it. Together with the forward control, the 1.5B teacher sweep (SP-RFT ≥ DPO at every teacher scale), and the gold-data control, both directions of the data-quality axis are now covered.

**M3 — Single-seed ablations.** We added seeds 123/456 to all three 7B full-FT factorial cells. At N=3: DPO 83.85 ± 0.85, SimPO 83.22 ± 1.41, SP-RFT 79.35 ± 0.77 — the inversion holds under full FT with a 4.5 pp DPO−SP-RFT separation, mirroring the LoRA-protocol cells (83.38/83.32/77.38) and closing the scale-vs-method confound with proper uncertainty. The revision reports the factorial as mean ± σ and flags any remaining single-seed cells explicitly.

**M4 — Mechanistic evidence beyond the narrative.** Two quantitative additions ground the RFT-ceiling account. (a) *Pass-rate composition across scales* (also the reviewer's Q4): the fraction of GSM8K training prompts yielding informative preference pairs falls monotonically with scale in every family — Qwen: 85.5% (0.5B) → 88.5% (1.5B) → 24.7% (7B) → 17.8% (14B) → 10.9% (32B); Gemma: 58.0% (1B) → 33.9% (12B); Llama-3: 89.1% (1B) → 51.0% (8B). The reviewer's suspicion is correct that effective dataset composition shifts with scale — this is inherent to the practitioner self-play workflow we benchmark, and it is exactly the confound the fixed-dataset, gold-data, and (new) reverse controls isolate. (b) *Item-level analysis at 1.5B* (3 seeds, per-item logs): format-error rates (flexible-correct ∧ strict-wrong) of 1.3–5.7% track strict-rank deficits, and strict solve-sets overlap heavily across algorithms (Jaccard 0.65–0.89) — methods largely solve the same items and differ at the margin, as the format-compliance regime predicts. We agree that gradient-norm / loss-landscape probes would be stronger still and scope them as future work in the Discussion.

**Weakness 3 — Shared hyperparameters for variants.** The conclusion is deliberately scoped to the *plug-and-play* setting (the revision states this explicitly): the claim is that variants provide no reliable out-of-the-box gains, not that tuned ceilings are equal. Two checks bound the risk: β sweeps for the two strongest 1.5B variants (DPOP: 54.7/55.2/53.3; ORPO: 54.8/54.4/53.8 at β ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 0.5}) move results ≤1.9 pp, and LR sweeps at 1.5B/3B/7B preserve the core rankings at every tested LR including per-algorithm optima.

**Q1 — Prompt-format sensitivity.** All evaluations use the identical LM-Eval-Harness prompt across families, so the evaluation side is held fixed; training data, however, is formatted with each model's own chat template (the practitioner workflow). We cannot fully deconfound template-algorithm interaction, and we will note this in Limitations. The strict-vs-flexible analysis bounds the concern at deployment scale: the 7B inversion persists under flexible extraction (+4.7 pp), so it is not an artifact of answer-format parsing.

**Q2 — LoRA rank at Gemma-12B.** Honest answer: r=16/α=32 was inherited from the Qwen setup, not separately validated for Gemma. The default LR was validated per-family only insofar as it matches both families' official fine-tuning guidance; a Gemma-side rank/LR sweep is noted as a limitation in the revision.

**Q3 — The MATH architecture reversal.** Our working hypothesis, stated as testable rather than established: SimPO's length-normalized, reference-free objective interacts with family-specific output-length and formatting distributions (Qwen-14B MATH responses are longer and benefit from length normalization; Gemma's stronger prior formatting favors reference-anchored updates). The item-level tooling released with the artifacts supports testing this; we prefer not to overclaim beyond the data.

**Q4 — Pass-rate distributions.** Yes — quantified above under M4(a); per-scale parquets with per-prompt outcomes are in the artifact repository.

We hope the discussion-period additions (reverse control, multi-seed factorial, IFEval, pass-rate quantification), together with the 32B extension and third model family reported in the other threads, give the reviewer confidence to advocate for the paper. We are happy to run any further check the reviewer considers decisive.
